# Rob Sand vs. Zach Lahn: Documented Positions on Iowa's 107 Framework Issues (Research Cutoff October 12, 2026)

Rob Sand, a Democrat serving his second term as State Auditor, and Zach Lahn, a Republican businessman and farmer running for office for the first time, are the confirmed major-party nominees for Iowa governor on November 3, 2026. Their documented records show sourced differences on Medicaid privatization, abortion, Education Savings Accounts, the minimum wage, income tax and school vaccine requirements. They overlap on opposing eminent domain for private gain, ending data center tax breaks, farmer incentives for water quality, more local control and universal school meals. On 29 of the 107 framework issues, neither candidate has a documented position.

## TL;DR

- **Who and what:** Sand (running mate Dave Muhlbauer) and Lahn (running mate state Rep. Derek Wulf) are the nominees. Lahn won a five-way Republican primary on June 2, 2026, defeating Trump-endorsed U.S. Rep. Randy Feenstra. Sand was unopposed. Sand has documented positions on more issues than Lahn. That is partly because Sand has published several detailed written plans and holds statewide office, while Lahn's platform page is shorter and built around goals. The gap reflects what material exists, not how hard each candidate was researched.
- **Clearest documented differences:**
  - Medicaid: Sand would end privatization "on day one"; Lahn would keep it with tighter accountability.
  - Abortion: Sand opposes the six-week ban and would veto further restrictions; Lahn says life begins at conception and wants to "continue to protect life."
  - ESAs: Sand wants income limits and audits; Lahn calls ESAs "a foundational freedom."
  - Minimum wage: Sand proposes a phased $12 with indexing; Lahn "wouldn't commit" to raising it (NBC paraphrase).
  - Taxes and vaccines: Lahn wants to eliminate the income tax over time and end school vaccine requirements; Sand says he would not reverse the recent income tax cuts.
- **What is missing:** Neither candidate has said whether he would:
  - sign HF 2104;
  - keep the December 1, 2026 Medicaid work requirement start date;
  - keep HF 2771's water treatment funding;
  - change SF 579, voter ID, early voting, public-sector bargaining (HF 291), HF 2527 or HF 2757.

  Three more debates (October 14, 21 and 27) may add positions after this cutoff.

**2. Documented overlap.** Each item below is a position both candidates stated themselves.
- **Eminent domain:** Both oppose its use for private gain. Neither has committed to a specific bill.
- **Data centers:** Both would end tax incentives and require data centers to cover their own costs.
- **Water quality and cancer:** Both rank these as top priorities and favor farmer incentives and more monitoring. Both oppose legal immunity for pesticide makers and support funding IWILL (Lahn's support for IWILL comes from secondary sources only).
- **Local control:** Both called for more of it at the October 6 debate.
- **Universal school meals:** Both support them.
- **Steel plant incentives:** Both say incentives should depend on results.
- **Schools:** Both set a goal of returning Iowa schools to the national top five.
- **Investor ownership:** Both propose curbs on investor ownership of land or homes.

**3. Documented differences**
- **Medicaid:** Sand would end privatized managed care. Lahn would keep it, citing accountability and a ban on AI denials of coverage.
- **Abortion and IVF:** Sand would veto further restrictions and protect IVF and contraception. Lahn holds that life begins at conception and says IVF needs regulation "to promote and protect life."
- **ESAs:** Sand wants income limits, audits, tuition caps and a "take the public" admission rule. Lahn defends the universal program, has said he would expand it, and backs third-party audits.
- **Income tax:** Sand would keep the 3.8% rate. Lahn wants it "to zero" over time.
- **Property tax:** Sand would let SF 2472 "play out." Lahn's long-term goal is to eliminate property taxes on primary residences.
- **Minimum wage:** Sand proposes a phased $12 with indexing. Lahn made no commitment.
- **Vaccines:** Lahn supports ending school vaccine requirements and backed an mRNA ban in January 2026.
- **Transgender issues:** Sand opposes SF 418's removal of gender identity from civil rights law, but also opposes transgender girls and women competing in female sports. Lahn has no documented SF 418 statement.
- **Emergency powers:** Lahn welcomes the new limits and "would likely sign more."

**4. Discrepancies found in the framework and in reporting**
- **ESA cost figures conflict.** The framework gives $8,148 per student in 2026-27. Iowa Starting Line reported "$5,400" per student and attributed a "$520 million" yearly cost to Sand. Sand's own plan cites "$350 million" for this school year. The $5,400 and $520 million figures are unverified.
- **Developments the framework misses:**
  - Reynolds signed a law limiting the next governor's emergency powers (Iowa Press, July 10, 2026).
  - In a one-day special session on October 2, 2026, which Reynolds signed the same day, lawmakers changed the MEGA program so the state can offer Mesabi Metallics "tax credits worth up to 10% of the investment into the plant over 10 years" for its proposed $15 billion Lee County steel plant (Iowa Public Radio). That comes to about $1.36 billion in incentives.
  - Reynolds announced a contract with Cognizant Government Solutions and Amazon Web Services on June 9, 2026. According to contracts The Gazette obtained through a public records request, the state "will pay an estimated $419.7 million over 10 years." The governor's office projects savings of "more than $525 million over 10 years" (governor.iowa.gov). The "half a billion" figure cited on Iowa Press matches the projected savings, not the contract's value.
- **Unverified rows:** The framework's Confidence L legal rows were not re-verified against the Iowa Code.

## Deliverable A: Candidate Policy Profiles

### Rob Sand

**How he describes his approach:** Divided government. "I'd say it's time for change, and I'm offering us a return to what Iowa is, people working together, even when they disagree" (October 6 debate, IPR). He stresses the limits of a governor facing a Republican legislature: "I feel like I have spent most of my life listening to politicians who promise that they're going to do stuff they can't do" (Daily Iowan, September 14, 2026). He also promises to veto "culture war bills."

**Published plans:** "Accountability for All," "A Healthier Iowa for All," "Making Life More Affordable," a cannabis plan, a data centers plan, and "Opportunities for All," which Radio Iowa (August 12, 2026) describes as a 15-point economic agenda.

**Major commitments:**
- **Medicaid:** End privatization on day one; raise reimbursement rates.
- **Schools:** A 5% increase in state aid in year one; universal full-day pre-K; universal school meals.
- **ESAs:** Income limits, annual audits and tuition caps.
- **Minimum wage:** $12, phased in, then indexed.
- **Cannabis:** Legalize and tax.
- **Cancer and water:** A cabinet-level cancer task force; $20 million for cancer research (matching Nebraska); higher tobacco and nicotine taxes; radon disclosure; water monitoring; a "dirty water" alert; conservation credits; wetlands; funding for IWILL.
- **Data centers:** Pay 100% of their own costs; no tax breaks; no NDAs.
- **Officeholder rules and elections:** Term and age limits; cognitive and civics tests; a stock trading ban; a top-four primary with approval voting.

**Official record (DOCUMENTED ACTION as auditor):**
- **ESA report (June 17 to 18, 2026):** Sand's ESA Policy Impact Report counts 41,892 nonpublic school students in FY2026, against 33,054 projected by the Department of Education in 2022. It attributes the difference of 8,838 students to ESAs. It puts total program cost at $329.6 million and says "taxpayers paid roughly $258.7 million in FY26 to fund private school tuition that otherwise would have been paid privately" (via WHO13 and Iowa Capital Dispatch; also The Gazette and CBS2).
- **Revenue warning:** Warned of an $816.6 million (8.4%) drop in FY2025 revenue (WOI).
- **PIE program:** Launched the Public Innovations and Efficiencies program.
- **Proposed legislation:** Proposed mandatory prison for theft of public funds "every year as State Auditor."
- **Medicaid audits:** Says his audits found more care denials after Medicaid was privatized. The underlying audit was not retrieved.

### Zach Lahn

**How he describes his approach:** "Iowa First," which puts Iowa families and Iowa-owned businesses ahead of "corporate interests, out-of-state investors, and political expediency" (zachlahn.com). He is anti-monopoly on agriculture and aligned with MAHA (endorsed by MAHA Action). He favors decentralization: "I want to make sure that we do not have centralized power as much as possible" (Iowa Press, July 10, 2026).

**Four-year goals:** Reverse youth outmigration, "10,000 new family farms," return to the top five in education, and move Iowa to the "bottom half of Cancer Diagnoses."

**Major commitments:**
- **Agriculture and land:** Antitrust suits against agricultural input companies, with more funding for the Attorney General; higher property tax rates on "Wall Street institutional investors"; confiscate land "illegally owned by China" and resell it to young families and veterans; ban hedge fund purchases of single-family homes.
- **Economic development:** End credits for out-of-state and foreign corporations.
- **Data centers:** No incentives; a fivefold property tax, then a moratorium.
- **Cancer and water:** Independent state product safety testing; refocus University of Iowa research; refundable credits for deep-banding fertilizer and aid for buffer strips.
- **Education:** "Innovation zones"; drastically reduced testing; revoke the licenses of teachers "caught pushing a political agenda."
- **Immigration:** No benefits for illegal immigrants; no H-1B hiring in state government or universities.
- **Youth retention:** An income tax abatement for young Iowans who stay, deposited into a down payment account.
- **First bill:** An "Iowa first food freedom bill," plus a goal that 80% of school food be grown in Iowa.

**Prior record (organizational):**
- **AFP Montana:** Helped lead AFP Montana's effort against Medicaid expansion (confirmed on Iowa Press).
- **Wonder school:** Co-founded it; Kansas Reflector (August 25, 2026) reports his personal loans sustain it.
- **Government record:** None.

## Deliverable B: Issue Comparison (All 107 Issues)

**Codes:**
- EP = explicit position
- SP = specific proposal
- DA = documented action
- GS = general statement
- IND = indirect evidence
- CF = conflicting evidence
- NDP = no documented position found in this review
- SNV = source not verified

"Debate" means the October 6 KWQC debate as reported by IPR, unless noted otherwise.

### Priority issues

**1.01 Property tax (SF 2472)**
- **Context:** SF 2472 caps most city and county levy growth at 2% and creates a homestead exemption of 10% of value, with a $5,500 minimum and $20,000 maximum.
- **Sand (EP):** Give the recent changes time to play out before adjusting them. He also said local officials "know their communities" (debate, paraphrase).
- **Lahn (EP and SP):**
  - Long-term elimination of property taxes on primary residences: "You shouldn't have to rent your home from the government" (debate).
  - Higher rates on institutional investors (site).
  - Data centers would "pay 5x the normal property tax rate," with the money going "to give property tax relief to Iowa residents" (X, February 19, 2026).
  - He has stated no plan for replacing local revenue.
- **Similarity:** Both favor local control.
- **Not addressed by either:** The cap rate, the exceptions, the EMS levy, or the state backfill.

**1.02 Income tax and SJR 11**
- **Sand (EP, CF):** "Those income cuts, I think, were reasonable. My criticism was not of the cuts, it was the fact that they have left us with a $1.3 billion budget deficit" (CBS2). His campaign told PolitiFact (September 17, 2026) he would not repeal the cuts. PolitiFact rated false Lahn's ad claim that Sand "wants to raise your taxes by $7,500 per year." See Deliverable F for his 2025 remarks.
- **Lahn (EP):** "I would like to find a way to get the income taxes to zero," paid for by cutting waste, and "No sales tax, no income tax, no property tax raises" (ITR Foundation summary). He gives no timeline.
- **SJR 11:** NDP for both.

**1.05 and 11.103 Budget and Taxpayer Relief Fund**
- **Sand (EP and DA):** "I would liken it to a fiscal time bomb. The fuse is the surplus" (Little Village). He proposes cannabis revenue.
- **Lahn (GS):** Find "ways that we're wasting money in government."
- **Neither:** Has a plan for the FY2027 gap.

**2.11 to 2.13 Farm economy and land ownership**
- **Sand (SP):** Higher taxes on out-of-state landowners; a ban on hedge funds and LLCs making large-scale land purchases; an "Office of Rural Prosperity" (Iowa Starting Line). He criticizes tariffs: "Farmers can't wait anymore" (debate).
- **Lahn (SP):**
  - "File antitrust suits against the big agricultural input companies that have formed monopolies and are extorting our farmers" (site), with more funding for the Attorney General (debate).
  - Confiscate land illegally owned by China.
  - Livestock diversification aid for fencing and water (debate).
- **Similarity:** Both would curb outside ownership.
- **Not addressed by either:** Iowa Code ch. 9H or ch. 10B by name, or how confiscation or antitrust suits would work legally.

**2.15 Eminent domain and CO2 pipelines**
- **Sand (EP):** "The idea that you would have your private property taken just because there's someone who thinks they can put it to better use, but it's not for public use, doesn't sit right with me" (KCAU).
- **Lahn (EP):** "Absolutely oppose use of eminent domain for private company profit" (site). "We'll see what the exact options are for that, and what the legality is of that... I think I can work with the Republican legislature" (KCAU).
- **Not addressed by either:** Whether he would sign HF 2104; the Utilities Commission (IUC) and Summit's permit; IUC appointment criteria; insurance; a severance tax.

**3.22 Medicaid and work requirements**
- **Sand (SP):** "As governor, I'll get started ending the privatization of Medicaid on day one of my administration" (robsand.com). Raise reimbursement rates. Supports the Work Without Worry bill, which would remove income and asset limits for working Iowans with disabilities (KTIV, October 8, 2026).
- **Lahn (EP):** "I don't think the government would do a good job of delivering healthcare for the people of Iowa... I will not allow any of these companies to use AI in denying coverage from people" (Iowa Capital Dispatch, August 1, 2026). On his AFP opposition to expansion: "those worries have largely been addressed" (Iowa Press).
- **Work requirements:** NDP for both on the December 1 start date, exceptions, and verification.

**3.24 Cancer**
- **Sand (SP):** A cabinet-level task force; $20 million for research; tobacco and nicotine taxes; nitrate alerts; radon disclosure by home inspectors (debate). He also points to lifestyle factors: "if we really want to tackle the cancer crisis, we need to look at all of those things" (debate).
- **Lahn (SP):** "Conduct independent state safety tests on products used in Iowa." Redirect University of Iowa research. Says environmental factors are "absolutely" involved, citing nitrates and glyphosate (Iowa Capital Dispatch, May 19, 2026). Expanded screenings (KCRG, October 7).
- **Similarity:** Both oppose immunity for pesticide makers.

**3.30 Vaccines**
- **Lahn (EP, CF):**
  - January 27, 2026: Radio Iowa reported he "would pull all COVID shots off the market in Iowa and end all childhood vaccine requirements connected to enrollment in Iowa schools."
  - September 2026, KCCI interview: answered "Yes" when asked if he would support eliminating school vaccine requirements. This comes from a transcript published by the Iowa Democratic Party (partisan); Studio 31 corroborates it.
  - Later, on CBS2: "My very firm belief is that there are benefits from vaccination, of course" and "Do I think that there's potential benefits from future mRNA technology? Of course there could be."
- **Sand (IND):** His campaign attacks Lahn's mRNA stance. HPV consent law: NDP.

**4.31 and 4.32 School funding and ESAs**
- **Sand (SP and DA):**
  - A 5% increase in state aid in year one (debate).
  - ESA rules: "Requiring annual audits," "Limiting tuition increases," "Implementing income limits," and ending the Odyssey arrangement (Accountability plan).
  - "The wealthiest folks in Iowa, God bless them, good for them, they don't need help to send their kids to private school" (debate).
  - "If you take public money, you take the public."
  - He is not proposing repeal: "I support Iowa families making the decision that's best for their kids."
  - Documented action: his auditor's ESA report.
- **Lahn (EP):**
  - "Education savings accounts are a foundational freedom for Iowa's families" (Iowa Press, July 10, 2026).
  - Would expand ESAs (Iowa Capital Dispatch, May 5, 2026, paraphrase).
  - Supports third-party audits (debate).
  - "The Governor Must Be the #1 Advocate for Public School Kids" (site).
- **Similarity:** Both support audits.
- **Differences:** Sand would add income limits and admission rules and has named a 5% aid figure. Lahn would keep eligibility universal and has named no aid figure.

**5.41 and 5.42 Abortion, contraception and IVF**
- **Sand (EP):** "I think that the six-week abortion ban goes too far... I can't unilaterally repeal a six-week ban, but I can stop us from making it worse" (Iowa Press, 2025). He supports the Roe framework and would veto restrictions "including IVF, surrogacy and contraception" (The Gazette, September 2026).
- **Lahn (EP):**
  - "Yes, I would like to continue to protect life in our state."
  - "If you believe that life begins at conception, then that decision is made for you."
  - On IVF: "I think we need to look at regulating that to promote and protect life" (Iowa Press, primary).
  - "We are now banned for life from that clinic because we refused to discard the embryos" (Radio Iowa, January 28, 2026).
- **SNV:** Lahn's position on exceptions for rape, incest, or the life of the mother; HF 2788; and a TIME report that he "called for a total abortion ban." These rest on partisan or unretrieved sources.
- **Not addressed by either:** Doctor liability.

**5.46 and 5.47 Gender identity, civil rights and sports**
- **Sand (EP):**
  - Opposes SF 418: "You should not be fired from your job in the state of Iowa for being different. You should not be evicted from your apartment" (The Gazette, 2025, via The Hill).
  - Says he could not restore the protections with a Republican legislature.
  - Opposes transgender girls and women in female sports. The quote "girls' sports are for girls and boys' sports are for boys" (Politico via Erin Reed) is secondary.
- **Lahn (IND):** Said at the debate that Sand "would allow a book on transgenderism to be put in an elementary school" (The Hill).
- **NDP for both:** SF 579 and facilities.

**6.51 to 6.53 Water quality**
- **Sand (SP):**
  - Restore water monitoring funding.
  - Income and per-acre property tax credits for farms with approved nutrient plans.
  - Verify manure plans, make manure records public, and fund digesters.
  - Wetlands and grazing land.
  - A "dirty water" alert.
  - Fund IWILL.
  - On regulation: in August 2025 he did not rule it out; in April 2026 he did not say (IPR).
- **Lahn (SP):**
  - "Give incentives to our farmers to use proven solutions."
  - Credits for deep-banding fertilizer; buffer strips.
  - "I believe that people want to do the right thing in the first place."
  - More monitoring.
  - "Clean water is a pro-life issue" (19th News).
- **Not addressed by either:** HF 2771 or Iowa Flood Center funding.

**6.59 and 11.101 Data centers**
- **Sand (SP):** Data centers "pay their own way, including covering any increase in costs on utilities so that your bills don't go up one cent." Also: "I will end these tax breaks," block NDAs, require union construction, and use integrated resource planning (IRP).
- **Lahn (SP, evolving):**
  - February 19: "Plan to pay 5x the normal property tax rate."
  - July 10: "I'm the candidate that's called for a moratorium on data centers within the state," yet "each county needs to look at what the unique situation in their county is"; "there should be no incentives."
  - August 24: regulation "at a state level" (Iowa Capital Dispatch).

**7.67 Immigration**
- **Sand (GS):** "If you are working, if you are following the rules, and have no criminal record, if you're paying your taxes, why would you want people who are doing those things to feel unsafe in the state of Iowa?" (town hall video, via Iowa Starting Line).
- **Lahn (SP):** "We will ban benefits for illegal immigrants, and stop issuing H1B visas for jobs that Iowans can do in state government and in our universities." State contracts would have to disclose how many Iowans they hire (Iowa Capital Dispatch, May 19, 2026).
- **NDP for both:** ICE cooperation, 287(g), E-Verify, SF 2340 and SF 2218. An Iowa GOP release calling Sand "silent" on ICE is partisan.

**8.71 Minimum wage**
- **Sand (SP):** Raise it from $7.25 to $12 "in phases," then index it (Radio Iowa, August 12, 2026). "You don't just flip the switch and say now it's $12" (Times-Republican, October 2026).
- **Lahn:** "Wouldn't commit to raise the minimum wage" (NBC News paraphrase of the debate).

**9.83 Audits**
- **Sand (SP and DA):** Restore "the full powers of the Auditor's Office" by reversing the 2023 law; make the PIE program mandatory; extend whistleblower protections to all Iowans.
- **Lahn (EP):** Third-party ESA audits. Accused Sand of politicizing the auditor's office. Position on the 2023 law: NDP.

## Deliverable D: Specific Proposals Catalog

**Sand:**
- **Medicaid:** End privatization starting on day one. No transition cost given.
- **Schools:** A 5% increase in state aid.
- **ESAs:** Income limits, audits and tuition caps. He says ESA reform would pay for school meals and pre-K "with millions of dollars leftover." No figure given.
- **Minimum wage:** $12, phased in and then indexed. No timeline.
- **Cannabis:** Regulated through the Alcoholic Beverages Division, with priority for grow licenses for small farms.
- **Cancer:** $20 million for research and a cabinet-level task force.
- **Caregiver credit:** His "Making Life More Affordable" plan proposes a "nonrefundable tax credit of up to $2,000 annually for caregivers, with an additional $1,000 for caretakers of veterans," targeted by income, and estimates "approximately 57,000 Iowans would be eligible." Radio Iowa (September 1, 2026) describes them as low-income Iowans who are the primary caregiver for an elderly or disabled spouse or relative.
- **Water:** Conservation credits, a "dirty water" alert and IWILL funding.
- **Data centers:** Cover their full costs and disclose their energy and water use.
- **Ethics:** A stock trading ban, term and age limits, required town halls enforced through ballot access, and a top-four primary with approval voting.
- **Housing:** Zoning reform and interest-free down payment aid.
- **Utilities:** Caps on utility fees.
- **Rural:** An Office of Rural Prosperity.

**Lahn:**
- **Taxes:** Phase out the income tax through efficiency savings (no timeline) and eliminate property taxes on primary residences over the long term. Raise property tax rates on institutional investors, and have data centers pay five times the normal property tax rate.

- **Economic development:** Redirect IEDA.
- **Youth retention:** An income tax abatement placed in a down payment account. The required years of residency are unspecified ("X amount of years").

- **Water:** Deep-banding credits.
- **Education:** Innovation zones, reduced testing and license revocation.
- **Immigration:** An H-1B ban in state government and universities, plus contractor disclosure of Iowan hires.

- **Data centers:** A moratorium.
- **Government:** More limits on emergency powers.

Neither candidate has published cost estimates.

## Deliverable F: Position Changes and Conflicting Statements

1. **Sand, income tax.**

3. **Lahn, data centers.** He has moved from a fivefold tax (February) to a moratorium plus county-by-county decisions (July) to state regulation (August). He has not said whether the fivefold tax still applies.

5. **Lahn, taxes.** He pledged "no property tax raises" but also proposes targeted increases on investors and data centers. He has not reconciled the two.
6. **Sand, transgender issues.** He opposes SF 418 and also supports sex-based sports restrictions. He presents these positions as consistent with each other.
7. **Sand, water regulation.** He did not rule it out in August 2025. His 2026 plan relies on incentives and is silent on regulation.
